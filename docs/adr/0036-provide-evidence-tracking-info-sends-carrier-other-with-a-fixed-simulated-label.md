# 0036. Provide evidence sends each tracking number as carrier `OTHER` with the fixed `carrier_name_other` "Carrier (simulated)"

Status: accepted, applies ADRs 0033 and 0035
Decided-by: answerer (inferred; the PayPal schema part is researched)
Question: T-008 (specs/features/11-dispute-evidence/tickets.md, "Dispute executors"): when the provide-evidence executor sends a `PROOF_OF_FULFILLMENT` item, what carrier does it send in `evidence_info.tracking_info[]`? The stored intent hashes tracking numbers but no carrier name.
Decision: For every tracking number in a stored evidence item, the executor sends `{ carrier_name: "OTHER", carrier_name_other: "Carrier (simulated)", tracking_number }`. The label is a single exported constant in the executor code. It is never read from the tracker or carrier records, and it is the same for every intent. An item with no tracking numbers (null or empty) sends no `tracking_info`.
Basis:
- PayPal Customer Disputes v1, `tracking_info`: `required: [carrier_name, tracking_number]`; `carrier_name_other` is "The name of carrier in free-form text for unavailable carriers. This field is mandatory when `carrier_name` is `OTHER`." (1 to 2000 characters, `^.*$`); `tracking_info_list` has `minItems: 1` and `maxItems: 10`. Sources: https://developer.paypal.com/api/customer-disputes/v1/schema.yaml and https://github.com/paypal/paypal-rest-api-specifications/blob/main/openapi/customer_disputes_v1.json (both read 23 Oct 2026). Sending `OTHER` with no `carrier_name_other` breaks the schema.
- Brief §8: "What you approve is what executes: an approval is single-use and bound to the hash of the exact intent." The variable values (types, notes, tracking numbers, refund IDs, documents) all come from the stored intent. A constant that's fixed in the executor (like `carrier_name: "OTHER"`) adds no unapproved record value, and approvals never re-read records (ADRs 0032, 0033).
- Brief §8: "Simulated parts are labeled on screen"; §10 trade-off 2, "Honest evidence: … simulation labeled"; §4 out of scope, "Real carrier integrations (mocked)". Every carrier in this demo is the mock, so "Carrier (simulated)" is always true and keeps the label requirements.md uses for the facts ("Carrier (simulated)") and the label rule ("Simulated carrier data is labelled in the facts, the PDF, the card and the template notes"). The carrier's own name still reaches PayPal through hashed paths: the notes ("Shipped with <carrier> …") and the PDF.
- Rejected: re-reading the tracker's carrier name, which would send an unhashed record value after approval. Also rejected: hashing carrier names in the intent, which would change the intent, the `provide_dispute_evidence` arguments and the `prepare_dispute_evidence` answer after T-006 and T-007 are done.
Consequences:
- The executor maps `trackingNumbers` to `trackingInfo` with this constant; the rail client's `DisputeTrackingInfo` is unchanged.
- The approval card (T-011) can show the constant next to the tracking numbers, so the card reads exactly what's sent.
- PayPal accepts at most 10 tracking entries per item, but the gate's proposal schema and the rail client allow 100. An item with more than 10 gets a 4xx and is recorded `failed`. That's fail-closed and nothing is retried, but capping at 10 in the proposal schema would refuse it earlier.
- If a real carrier integration replaces the mock, revisit this: the intent would then need to hash a carrier code per tracking number.
